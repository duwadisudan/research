Sudan Duwadi · PhD candidate, Biomedical Engineering, Boston University

I study how people listen when everyone is talking at once.

I work in the Sen and Boas labs at Boston University. I measure the brain with light, run listening experiments with competing talkers, and build machine learning models on speech and physiological data. These are the three main projects of my PhD years. Each page starts from zero and lets you try the experiment yourself.

The cap is drawn from the real layout: 56 light sources, 144 detectors. Point at a talker to attend to that side.

What the projects share

The three projects share one idea: the standard measurement misses something. The sound at the ears does not say which voice the listener is following, a word score does not say what the listening cost, and a transcript does not say how something was said. In each project I try to measure the missing part, from the brain, the body, or the voice.
